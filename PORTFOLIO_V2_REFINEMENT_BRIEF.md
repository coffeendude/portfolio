# AndrewBrought.com v2 — Refinement Brief

## Current assessment

The redesign is moving in the right direction, but it currently reads as a polished technical portfolio template rather than an unmistakable founder/operator platform.

The strongest element is the hero positioning. The weakest elements are the generic card grid, placeholder visual language, invented or unverified proof statements, and a contact section that feels like a consulting landing page.

The next iteration should create hierarchy, proof, personality, and strategic specificity.

## What to preserve

- The near-black / deep navy foundation
- The restrained teal accent
- The editorial serif + sans-serif pairing
- The headline direction around technology, product, and growth
- The three-part operating range:
  - Build the foundation
  - Shape the product
  - Create momentum
- The stage-to-systems narrative
- The simplified navigation

## What must change

### 1. Remove all unverified or invented proof

Immediately remove any claim that has not been supplied and verified by Andrew.

Examples currently visible that must be removed unless explicitly verified:
- “Reduced MTTR by 45%”
- “Designed and launched an analytics pipeline for product insights”
- “Built integrations and partner playbooks to accelerate adoption”
- any title, metric, client, partner, result, funding, or traction not grounded in Andrew’s source material

Use neutral placeholders in source data only:
`[VERIFY OR REPLACE]`

Never render placeholders in the production interface.

### 2. Replace the generic project-card grid

The current three equal cards create a bootcamp/SaaS-template feel.

Replace them with an editorial “Selected Work” layout.

#### Featured case study
A large, horizontally composed feature for **Vigour**.

Include:
- Status: `Venture in development`
- One-sentence thesis
- Andrew’s actual operating role
- What is being built
- Current strategic work:
  - platform
  - vigorOS
  - hardware
  - manufacturing path
  - product philosophy
  - capital strategy
- A clear “Read the case study” or “View working thesis” action

Use real Vigour visuals or restrained typographic art, not a generic icon.

#### Secondary case studies
Two smaller but substantive entries:
- Platform & Data Systems
- Technical GTM & Founder Operations

Each should show:
- situation
- Andrew’s role
- decision
- work
- verified result
- what it demonstrates

Do not use equal-height generic cards. Use asymmetric editorial composition.

### 3. Add a stronger “Now” section

Directly under the hero, add a concise current-focus strip:

## What I’m building now

- **Vigour** — intentional technology across software, operating systems, and hardware
- **AI infrastructure and governed systems** — production readiness, agency, reliability, and control
- **Technical founder operations** — product, GTM, partnerships, and company-building

This section should establish current momentum before historical proof.

### 4. Make the site more personal and less templated

The current design is competent but anonymous.

Add:
- one current professional portrait
- one high-quality stage image
- one real working image or product artifact
- a restrained visual motif inspired by performance:
  - rhythm
  - cue lines
  - timing marks
  - stage-light gradients
  - editorial captions

Do not bring back:
- autoplay video
- eye-shaped media
- Matrix imagery
- generic code visuals
- floating 3D shapes
- decorative technology icons

### 5. Refine the hero

Keep the positioning, but increase specificity and reduce generic phrasing.

Recommended hero:

Eyebrow:
`TECHNICAL FOUNDER-OPERATOR`

Headline:
`I turn complex technology into systems, products, and companies that move.`

Alternative:
`I build the systems, products, and operating strategy behind ambitious technology companies.`

Body:
`I bring platform engineering depth, product judgment, technical GTM, and founder operating experience to early-stage companies where technical capability must become commercial momentum.`

Primary CTA:
`Explore selected work`

Secondary CTA:
`Read my thinking`

Tertiary text link:
`Start a conversation`

Under the hero, use a proof line:
`Platform Engineering · Distributed Systems · Product Strategy · Technical GTM · Founder Operations`

Avoid centering every element. Use a more editorial two-column composition with portrait or visual artifact.

### 6. Turn “Thinking” into real public proof

The current thinking cards read like sample content.

Replace with real or clearly labeled working pieces:
- The Vigour Manifesto
- Living Deliberately — Founder Thesis
- AI, Agency, and Governed Systems
- Infrastructure as Product Strategy
- From Performer to Founder-Operator

Each item should include:
- title
- one-sentence thesis
- status: Essay / Working thesis / Manifesto / Field note
- publication date or “In progress”
- real link or hidden draft state

No “Read more” link should exist unless the destination exists.

### 7. Rebuild “From stage to systems”

Current copy is too generic and the image is a placeholder.

Use a strong split editorial section.

Headline:
`From stage to systems.`

Body:
`The stage taught me that every complex outcome is both a system and a story. Performance required preparation, timing, adaptation, trust, and the ability to read a room in real time. Engineering taught me how to design the infrastructure behind reliable outcomes. Founder work taught me how to decide which outcomes are worth building.`

Supporting line:
`Today, I work across those disciplines—building systems, shaping products, and helping teams translate technical possibility into company-level results.`

Use:
- one excellent performance image
- one current portrait or working image
- optional short caption beneath each

Remove generic bullet statements unless those ideas are integrated into real prose.

### 8. Simplify the contact section

The current contact section feels like a consulting funnel.

Remove:
- “I’ll respond within 3 business days”
- broad availability claims
- generic “How can I help?” language
- the oversized decorative logo

Recommended:

# Let’s build something consequential.

`I’m most interested in founder, product, technical leadership, AI infrastructure, and venture-building conversations where technical depth must translate into company-level outcomes.`

Actions:
- Email Andrew
- Discuss a role
- Explore a venture
- Invite me to speak

Keep the form only if it is functional and valuable.
Otherwise, use email + scheduling link + LinkedIn.

### 9. Improve visual hierarchy

Current problem:
Too many sections use the same dark card treatment.

Use three visual modes:

#### Editorial dark
Hero, stage-to-systems, closing

#### Warm reading surface
Thinking / essays / thesis excerpts

#### Structured operating surface
Selected work and operating range

Introduce:
- warm off-white
- muted brass or soft amber as a secondary accent
- subtle teal continuity
- wider reading measures
- fewer borders
- fewer equal cards
- more asymmetry
- more negative space

### 10. Update the logo

The current icon looks generic and low-resolution.

Use one of:
- simple `AB` monogram
- Andrew’s existing handwritten `A` mark, cleaned and simplified
- wordmark only: `Andrew Brought`

Do not use a generic abstract technology icon.

## Revised homepage structure

1. Header
2. Hero
3. What I’m building now
4. Operating range
5. Featured work: Vigour
6. Secondary case studies
7. Thinking
8. From stage to systems
9. Contact
10. Footer

## Acceptance criteria

- The page feels like an executive founder platform, not a portfolio template.
- The first 15 seconds establish Andrew’s present positioning.
- Vigour is the featured current proof.
- No unverified metrics or claims appear.
- Real writing replaces placeholder thought-leadership cards.
- The performer background is strategic, not decorative.
- The design has hierarchy and asymmetry.
- Real images replace generic icons.
- Contact feels selective and human.
- All links work.
- The build passes.
- The site remains accessible and responsive.
